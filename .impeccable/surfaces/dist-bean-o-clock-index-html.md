## Scope

The single-page storefront for Bean O' Clock Coffee To-Go (first client demo). Visitor mode: Persuade. V4 (2026-11-09) is a client-directed brand-experience rework: "a playful, premium, modern food-and-beverage brand identity that feels like a complete creative world", away from V3's brutalist wide capitals and static poster blocks, with ordering kept whole. References (principles only, nothing copied): Fika House (one system repeated across every touchpoint), MUVIN (a line-art character that lives with photography), OFFBRiEF (confidence, character-led humour).

## Audience and job

Tagum visitors on phones. They decide whether to order ahead for pickup or get delivery, and want to see what to try and what it costs. The client also uses the page to show creative production capability. Intended sequence: "this shop has a personality", then "this looks good", then "what should I order?", then "I can order it here".

## Direction contract

THESIS (V4): Coffee around the clock, as everyday life rather than a clock gimmick. THE CHARACTER: a small line-drawn barista whose head is the logo's own solid bean (crease and face cut out), in a crema apron, a watch on its wrist, a to-go cup in hand. Time lives in its props and its day (the watch, the cup, the walk), never in its body. It is a rig (brand.js): it walks, raises a cup, sips, checks the watch, presents, sits, leans, waves, shrugs, carries a tray, tamps, pulls a lever, pours, rides a scooter; since V7 it leans at the waist. Used once per section at most, never as decoration on its own: it sets the clock to bean o'clock (hero), sits on the product (the usual), holds each list's cup on the board (menu), checks the time with an order in hand (order), rides to the shop (visit), leans on the sticker (footer), and keeps you company in the drawer.

OWN-WORLD (V4): Colour: logo green #516752 and cream #F6F1EB, deep green #3B4D3C, espresso #2E211A for one dark ground and for text (ink #2B231D); crema #CF8A45, the colour of the foam, is the one accent (the apron, cup sleeves, stamps, full stops, the line that runs through the page), never running text. Type: Fraunces at its softest (SOFT 100), sentence case, warm and round-shouldered, kin to the flared serif on the shop's own "New drink!" posters, with its italic as the brand's aside; Figtree, a friendly geometric sans like the logo's lettering, for everything functional; tracked capitals only for small labels, as the logo sets "COFFEE TO-GO". One pen: lines of one weight that keep their weight at any size, round ends; solid shapes only for the bean head, hands, shoes and crema. Motifs, used selectively: the minute track (the logo's dial unrolled into a strip of ticks, the brand's border); the round stamp (words around a ring, turning); the shine (three dial ticks beside a product); line-art cups for each category; the posters' coffee branch; a hand-drawn arrow. Shapes: the logo's sun-over-a-hill, arches, discs, die-cut stickers, packaging labels; pill buttons with a printed edge that press down.

STORY (V4): brand, character, type, photography, product, motion, order. Hero: the logo rebuilt as a scene (dial ticks, the coffee rising behind the hill like the logo's sun, EST. and 2020 on the ground, the stamp) and the barista walking in to raise a cup. The cup sleeve (menu facts in the italic, each with its cup, between two minute tracks). The usual (the pour under an arch, the barista sitting on it, the crema line arriving from the hero to draw its frame; three best sellers). New on the board (the shop's own poster drinks as die-cut stickers, a stamp, shines, the branch). What goes in the cup, on espresso (arch, disc, arch; the line runs through them; Tamp. Pull. Pour.; the boards' claims as packaging labels). The whole board (V6: one board, a list at a time; tags with cup marks, a green plate with the list's picture and barista, the list on paper). Pick a time (the barista checks the watch; the watch face, blown up in crema, keeps the time in Tagum). This way (the barista walks in on a dotted route and points to the pin). Footer sign-off, the barista leaning on the stamp.

FIRST VIEWPORT (V8): A green field ending on a horizon, not an edge. Left: "It's bean o'clock." in Fraunces (bean in the italic, crema stop), lede, "Order ahead" and "Get it delivered", rating and starting price. Right, no photograph: the Timekeeper. The logo's dial on its ground (open ring, minute ticks, a steaming crema cup in place of twelve), the barista life-size, leaning in and pushing the crema minute hand up toward the cup, a takeaway cup in the other hand, EST. and 2020 at the ground's ends, over a faint paper of beans, tiny clocks and cups that clears round it. The cup sleeve shows below.

FORM: The client's own logo, menu boards and posters, pinned by the brief. seed: none (no roll; the world is pinned by the client's brief and assets). V4 is set by the user's brief ("Do not ask for approval before making the creative decisions"); the result is pending the user's review.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.

## Signature interaction and motion

Seven verbs, used the same way everywhere: reveal (a headline rises in its line), mask (a photograph opens out of its arch or disc), draw (a line is drawn by the pen), scale (a sticker or stamp is slapped on with overshoot), slide (the sleeve travels with the scroll), drift (steam rises, stamps turn), transform (the barista changes pose).
- Hero, one sequence (V8): the two lines rise; the ground and the dial's ring and ticks are drawn on; the barista pushes the minute hand up from a quarter to toward twelve and the cup at twelve pops on; EST. and 2020 appear; the paper comes up underneath. Then, while on screen, a 4s breath-and-push loop and a slow drift of the paper.
- The thread (wide screens): one crema line, drawn by the scroll, comes out of the ground beside the cup, loops once like a pen flourish, runs down beside the usual's arch and becomes its frame.
- The usual: the barista on the arch takes a sip every few seconds.
- Craft: arch, disc and arch open in turn; the crema line is drawn through them.
- Order: as the band arrives the barista raises its wrist to read the watch.
- Visit: the barista walks in, stops, points; the dotted route runs out and the pin pops.
- Drawer: shrugs at an empty cart, checks the watch at the pickup time, raises a cup to a placed order.
- Feedback: the used + turns to a check with three crema ticks shining out; the cart count bumps; the board's crema minute track slides under the picked tag.
- The board (V6): picking a list rolls the two titles through their line like a sign board's letters, swaps the drinks with a short slide from the side of travel (nothing staggers: it is used again and again), slaps on the list's picture and turns its barista to its pose; about 400ms.
- V4 finish review (2026-11-09): disposition fix. Applied: the hero kicker removed (Tagum City moved into the lede); the footer stamp's centre changed from the bean to a cup so it cannot read as a redrawn logo seal; the thread now finishes as the arch's frame (drawn earlier on the scroll, the empty band above the usual tightened); the craft claims die-cut as three different labels (hang tag, oval jar label, arch-topped bag label); the craft statement set at lede scale; the order barista's pose changed to reading its own watch; the button edge made a 2px darker tone of the button's own fill. Not applied: a logo raster in the footer (the green tile clashes on the deep footer, as in V3; the shop's vector is owed) and a post-hoc concept seed (no roll shaped this direction; recording one would misstate it).
- Hover: buttons lift on their printed edge and press down; stickers straighten; labels straighten and lift; photographs zoom slightly. Everything rests under reduced motion; the figures are drawn in their final poses without JS.

## V5 refinement (2026-11-09): scale, pacing, composition

Client-directed; the identity is unchanged. A 1160px container (820px narrow) gives a 1366px screen deliberate margins. The hero is about two-thirds of the viewport (522px at 1366x768) with the sleeve showing beneath; type, the sun, section padding, Visit, the watch face and the footer sign-off all step down. The usual's arch is 4:5 and four columns wide. "What goes in the cup" no longer uses photographs: the barista performs the process in three drawn scenes (tamp, pull while timing the shot on its watch, pour with a heart blooming), played in turn once as the section arrives; on a phone each scene sits beside its words. The three tamp/pull/pour stand-in photographs no longer ship. Menu, ordering, visit and the labels are kept. Page length at 1366: 10,134px to 8,686px.

## V6 (2026-11-10): the menu board and the enhanced photographs

Client-directed; the identity is unchanged. The menu no longer stacks its five lists down the page (3,293px at 1366): it is one board, 944px with its heading. Five tags (each list's cup, name and drink count, stuck on by hand; the picked one green and straight, the crema minute track under it) sit over a stage that is 16:9 of the 1160px wrap at 1440x900, trimmed to 1160 by 592 at 1366x768 so the tags and the whole board are seen at once. The stage is a green plate (the list's number, title, note and count; its picture; the barista holding that list's cup) beside the list on paper, set after the shop's own boards: two columns, group labels, dotted leaders, the coffee branch faint behind. Every list shares the stage's one cell, so it never changes size. Each list's picture: the espresso print (enhanced photograph), the salty cream stamp, and the shop's own Matcha Oreo and Strawberry Oreo posters as stickers that add the drink. On a phone the tags ride under the nav and scroll sideways, and only the picked list is in the page. The menu head's separate barista is gone: the board's is the section's one figure. Rows, prices, add buttons, the item sheet and the order flow are unchanged; links to #cat-<list> (the craft labels) pick that list.

The enhanced photographs (compiled-photos/Enhanced Photos) are generated stand-ins for the library photographs. Used where there was a clear match: the overhead cappuccino in the hero disc, the pour in the usual's arch, the espresso into a glass on the board's espresso plate; cropped to their slots and served as WebP at two sizes (hero 760/460, pour 1024/560, glass 720/420). The bottomless pull and the tamp are kept with the client's images as reserves (no slot uses them since V5). The milk-steaming photograph was not used: the watch in it shows a legible third-party brand. Credits name the generated images as generated for the demo and not the shop. The craft band's gaps were trimmed a little (1,145px to 1,106px). Page length at 1366: 8,686px to 6,298px.

## V7 (2026-11-10): the character system, the process film, the product photographs

Client-directed; the identity is unchanged. Every appearance of the barista now does something, and no two alike: raises a cup (hero), sits and sips (the usual), tamps, pulls, pours (craft), holds each list's cup and carries the extras on a swaying tray (the board), checks the watch with a pickup bag in hand (order), rides a scooter in a half-face crema helmet (visit), waves goodbye (footer). The rig gained a waist joint, an elbow that bends up or down by reach, a helmet and new props (tray, bag); a hand's place is computed the same way on the server and in the browser, so the lever machine's lever stays in its hand.

What goes in the cup is three frames of one film at one counter, each readable without its word: TAMP leans in and presses the tamper into the basket (the mound of grounds goes in; a crema arrow, impact ticks); PULL leans back and brings a lever machine's lever down from overhead, the same portafilter locked in, the shot running into the cup; POUR stands tall, hand on hip, jug held out and tipped, milk into the same cup, a heart on the crema. Played once as the section arrives (about 4.6s); drawn done without motion. The Pull note now describes the lever ("Lever down: a single or double shot runs into the cup.").

Visit: the scooter rolls in a short way, the route runs out to the pin, then it idles and revs on a 4s CSS loop (wheels, road, suspension buzz, a rev with a puff, speed lines and a head tip) that runs only while on screen. Small moments: the add-ons tray sways, the board's barista hops when its drink is added, the order barista takes a second look at the watch on hover, the footer barista waves once. Reduced motion rests all of it.

New on the board: the three poster drinks now use enhanced versions of the shop's own photographs (same drinks, glasses and tables), cropped square with each glass at the same size and the colour kept near the posters'; 640 and 360px WebP. The poster crops are kept in assets/source/. One enhanced file is misnamed ("Caramel Cookie Crumb Iced Latte" is the White Chocolate with Oreo Cream: same glass, coaster and table as the poster).

## V9 (2026-11-10): the clock moves, every barista answers

Client-directed interaction pass; identity, layout, menu and ordering unchanged. The hero clock's hands are physical: a press is a push into angular velocity, friction brings it back to a slow creep, fixed-step integration keeps it identical at any frame rate and stable under any number of presses (velocity capped twice). The minute hand is light and quick, the hour hand about four times heavier. The barista holds the minute hand until it is knocked free, follows it round with its eyes, glances at its watch while it waits, and catches it when it comes back up slowly within reach, then sets it to its place again; tapped, it shoves the hand once round the dial (and catches it) or, while waiting, shrugs. The cup at twelve hops as the minute hand passes. Every other barista has one act of its own on a press (toast; tamp, pull, pour done again; offer the list's drink; lift the syrup bottle off the tray; right on time with the bag; rev the scooter; wave goodbye), on one set of beats, queued at most once. All are keyboard buttons; reduced motion keeps them usable (instant hand steps, a fade). Idles stay where they were: the hero, the usual's sip, the scooter, the tray. The one-time nudge: on a coarse pointer, a visitor who has never played with the clock sees once that it moves — after 5s on screen untouched the barista gives the minute hand two little pushes under a faint cream glow, then it is theirs; remembered in local storage.
